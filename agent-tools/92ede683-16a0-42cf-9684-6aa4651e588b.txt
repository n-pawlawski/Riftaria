# Guide: Class setups

This is a Guide page. This means the page will walk you through a specific task, strategy, or enemy/ boss fight.

Status: Under revision (This Guide is currently being revised or has been revised partially. It has not met all quality standards yet.)

This is the guide that relates to the Desktop version, Console version, and Mobile version. For the guide that relates to the Old-gen console version and Nintendo version, see Legacy:Guide:Class setups.

For detailed armor recommendations at every stage of the game, see Guide:Armor progression. 

"Setup" redirects here. For the game mechanic, see Loadouts. 

Terraria has no formal player class or leveling system. However, weapons can be grouped into four distinct categories based on their damage type — melee, ranged, magic, and summoner. Alongside weapons, several armor sets and accessories operate in tandem to these and potentially can only benefit specific damage types and/or playstyles. Each class has its strengths and weaknesses and has a wide variety of weapons to choose from. 

Melee

The melee class sports the highest overall survivability bonuses [a] of any class across progression as well as decent crowd control, but many melee weapons are limited in range. Although short-ranged melee weapons tend to deal more damage and are less of a challenge to use with the class' high defense, those that fire projectiles are favored especially later in progression, where the former weapons require specialized defensive builds to use effectively. 

While melee has the highest buffs to defense and is the only class to specialize in increasing the player's health regeneration, other damage types do not, in contrast, specialize in stats like boosting mobility or improving evasiveness in exchange for their lower overall survivability (with very few exceptions in the lategame). Instead, they compensate in the form of direct damage increases. Furthermore, offensive bonuses are not nearly as valuable as defensive bonuses, as time constraints in the perspective of a bossfight are rarely pertinent. As a result, this leads to specific "tank" builds that can utilize melee armor pieces, which sacrifice buffs to the class's respective damage in favor of survivability bonuses, being viable in the late game. 

Ranged

Rangers are able to attack from a distance, doing high single-target, piercing, or splash damage. They most often utilize weapons like bows and guns, although more atypical forms of damage (referred to as "specialist" weapons) such as throwing weapons, weapons that can use darts, or others can also be relevant at specific points in progression. Ranged users must constantly acquire ammunition, unless using the Endless Quiver or Endless Musket Pouch or using a ranged weapon that doesn't require ammunition such as the Toxikarp or Paintball Gun. 

Magic

Mages are fragile but highly varied in attack style, often utilizing special mechanics such as homing, lifesteal, and area-of-effect damage. They are held back by reliance on mana, which effectively increases their attack efficiency (and therefore their DPS), since when expended, magic weapons become slower to use. Magic users can often sustain their mana using Mana Stars when fighting many enemies at once, which also grants the Mana Surge buff. A vast majority of magic weapons are specialized for crowd control, with especially strong single-target weapons only being unlocked far later in progression. 

Summon

Summoners deal most of their damage using autonomous minions and/or sentries, and their attacks usually have excellent accuracy. However, most summoning armor sets have low defense in exchange for their high damage potential. Alongside minions and sentries, summoners can deal active damage in the form of whips. These are primary weapons that focus summons onto specific targets and can increase their damage output. Whips cause minions to switch targets to whichever enemy was last struck, which can be undesirable when fighting bosses that spawn smaller enemies. In such cases, it is often ideal to manually set one's minions to target the boss, and use any weapon except whips. 

Summoners can gain synergistic benefits from hybridizing with other classes: 

- As stated earlier, melee armor pieces can provide higher survivability when using melee armor pieces in hybrid sets. On top of this, certain melee accessories (chiefly the Feral Claws and its upgrades) improve the speed of whips; some melee armor pieces can do this, too.
- Both ranged and magic weapons are useful for supplementing longer-range damage in situations when whips would be less optimal. Notably, when using minions that deal damage to enemies up-front (rather than projectile-based minions) with these damage setups, the player is able to deal damage at a consistent rate while being able to maintain their distance.

General

Each class gains more complex abilities as the game progresses, enabling more strategic variety in combat. It is advised for players to try out every weapon they find to help determine what works best for them. Although not required for anything, it is strongly encouraged to purchase a DPS Meter which can be bought from the Traveling Merchant, and to test your weapons on either Target Dummies or easy bosses that you have already killed and can summon multiple times (some weapons, like minions, have issues when the player attacks Target Dummies). Always be curious and, whenever possible, try to seek out new weapons or synergies with accessories that you think might be better than your current loadout; never strictly lock yourself to one type of class damage for an entire playthrough (unless you are doing a challenge). This also helps to gauge your progress throughout the entire playthrough. Furthermore, it is also encouraged to use buff potions and healing potions as they broadly increase efficiency and survivability in combat (especially during events and boss fights) with little to no downside. 

In general, it is recommended to focus on gear that benefits one class. This is because equipment slots are limited, and many armors and accessories only benefit a specific class, especially later into the game. Summoners are usually an exception to this rule, since summons can deal damage alongside melee/ranged/magic weapons, so summoning gear will always provide a benefit as long as on-tier summons are being used. All classes can use at least two minions and two sentries at all times even without using summoning gear or potions (by using a Bewitching Table or War Table), so it's recommended to always utilize these free slots regardless of primary class for what is essentially free damage. Despite these weapons belonging to the distinct summon damage type, these weapons were balanced around the idea of the player also using the strongest normal weapon available in tandem with them, which includes either whips or melee/ranged/magic damage. [1] Using minion slot-boosting accessories can even be viable for non-summoners, if the player relies on percentage-based damage increases that apply to all damage classes, or is using mixed armor sets that both benefit their respective type of damage and summon damage. 

Note that although it is beneficial to generally focus on one class between melee/ranged/magic at a time, there is no real benefit to specializing in exclusively one class over the course of an entire playthrough, and one should strongly consider switching classes if they happen to obtain a powerful weapon from a different class. Additionally, off-class weapons can and will be useful in situations where one's main class weapons are ineffective. Some examples of this would be using whips against enemies that can pass through walls where bullets can't reach, using swords against enemies where long-range damage is not as effective, or using things like bows, guns, or magic weapons against enemies that can't be reached by swords. Compared to a traditional role-playing game, Terraria's classes are not set in stone, and the game allows for a large degree of freedom in which it is not nearly as punishing if off-class weapons are used. ## Pre-Bosses [edit]

At this stage, focusing on a class can be more beneficial since armor sets have class benefits. Many accessories still buff the player in general rather than one class, so using the most powerful weapon you find rather than one class is still very beneficial. Once a Shadow Orb or Crimson Heart has been broken and you have obtained more than 200 health, there is a chance every new day (at 4:30 AM) for the Goblin Army to invade, which eventually unlocks the Tinkerer's Workshop from the Goblin Tinkerer, being able to be used to upgrade and combine your accessories. Alternatively, you may also summon the army with a Goblin Battle Standard once you reach 200 health (which can be used before any Shadow Orbs or Crimson Hearts have been destroyed).

## Pre- mechanical bosses [edit]

At this point in the game, damage bonuses from armor and accessories become more significant, so it is encouraged at this point to choose a class to specialize in. Ranger is generally agreed to have the best weapons at this stage. However, melee also improves significantly due to melee armors having significantly higher defense than any other class. Magic is still heavily focused on crowd control, making it an effective class for those who want to take out The Destroyer first. Summoners have their best weapons locked behind Dreadnautilus and Queen Slime, and as both bosses can present a challenge if the player is using a summoner loadout, it may be worth switching to a different loadout temporarily to deal with them. If either of these hurdles are passed, it is also recommended to mix your chosen class with summoner at this stage, if you are running a primarily offensive loadout. 

This is also the point where a fully-defensive build begins to diverge from damage builds thanks to the availability of the Flesh Knuckles and Star Veil coupled with greater access to damage accessories, so it is a good time to decide whether you want to run a primarily offensive or defensive loadout and reforge your accessories accordingly.

## Pre- Plantera [edit]

At this point, players are expected to have chosen between melee, ranged, magic, summoning, or a hybrid of summoning and another class. This is also where significant armor variety starts to show up within the same class, with players choosing between Hallowed armor, Chlorophyte armor, and tier 2 Old One's Army armor. For magic and ranged, the choice lies between Hallowed armor (for offensive loadouts in need of a defensive effect) or Chlorophyte armor (for all-out offense). Melee users should use Turtle armor for pure defense, or some combination of Hallowed/Chlorophyte Mask, Monk's Shirt/Squire's Plating, and Squire's Greaves for the highest overall stats. If a reliable source of Defender Medals is not available, full Chlorophyte armor with the Chlorophyte Mask also works for offensive melee sets, and for defensive ranged/magic sets. 

As was the case in previous stages, ranged provides the highest long-distance DPS, melee provides the best tanking ability, magic provides the most versatile weapon effects, and summoner provides the most effortless mob clearing.

## Pre- Golem [edit]

With the Hardmode Dungeon and the final version of the Solar Eclipse now available, players have access to a variety of new equipment. Furthermore, any Biome Keys you have obtained are now usable. You could attempt to bypass this stage by killing the Empress of Light, Duke Fishron, or summoning the Frost Moon/ Pumpkin Moon, but most players will find it easier and more logical to kill Golem first. 

The transition from pre-Plantera to post-Plantera comes with a significant improvement to the equipment available, with the most prominent new items being melee weapons (Terra Blade, Vampire Knives) and magic armor (Spectre armor). With the exception of the Terra Blade providing near-endgame levels of power, the classes are otherwise very balanced at this stage.
